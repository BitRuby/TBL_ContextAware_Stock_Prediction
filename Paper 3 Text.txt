1. Introduction

1. Introduction
The rapid growth of the Internet and digital communication has significantly transformed the way information is exchanged globally. Every day, millions of people share their knowledge, discuss world events, and express their opinions on social media platforms. As a result, social networks play an increasingly influential role in shaping investor sentiment and decision-making in financial markets.

Investors now frequently rely on social media to gauge market sentiment and anticipate stock movements. Platforms such as X (formerly Twitter), Facebook, and Reddit facilitate financial discussions, where users engage under specific hashtags to exchange thoughts on market trends, corporate earnings, and economic policies. This growing reliance on social media has positioned it as a potential leading indicator for stock market trends. However, the challenge remains in determining how to best extract, process, and integrate this information into predictive models.

Natural Language Processing (NLP) plays a crucial role in extracting meaningful insights from this vast amount of textual data. Sentiment analysis, in particular, enables the identification of market mood and its correlation with stock price fluctuations. Recent advancements in NLP, especially Transformer-based models, have significantly improved the accuracy of sentiment extraction, making it a valuable tool for financial forecasting.

However, while prior research has explored sentiment extraction for stock prediction, many studies overlook key feature engineering aspects—such as engagement-weighted sentiment, cross-stock sentiment influence, and distinguishing between generic vs. stock-specific sentiment—which may significantly impact model performance. Furthermore, a major limitation in existing research is the lack of transparency in dataset construction. Many studies use small, proprietary, or poorly documented datasets, making replication difficult and leaving uncertainty regarding data representativeness.

This study addresses these gaps by:
✅ Constructing a large-scale, openly described dataset containing thousands of financial posts across multiple stocks and timestamps, ensuring robust and reproducible analysis.
✅ Investigating whether engagement-weighted sentiment scores improve stock price forecasting.
✅ Examining cross-stock sentiment influence (e.g., whether sentiment about Nvidia impacts Google stock prices).
✅ Comparing generic (#investing) vs. stock-specific ($GOOGL) sentiment signals in reinforcement learning models.
✅ Analyzing how sentiment volatility correlates with stock price fluctuations.

To achieve this, we compile a large-scale dataset of financial Twitter posts from 01-01-2020 to 01-01-2025, covering multiple stock-related hashtags (#btc, $NVDA, $GOOGL, $AMZN, $AAPL, $MSFT, $TSLA, $SPX, and #investing). This dataset spans thousands of posts across all hours of the day, enabling an in-depth analysis of intraday sentiment shifts. We extract sentiment scores using the DeepSeek model and integrate them into a Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) framework for stock price forecasting.

To evaluate model effectiveness, we assess cumulative profit and win rate as primary performance metrics. Additionally, we apply Granger causality tests to determine whether sentiment serves as a leading or lagging indicator and use SHAP analysis to quantify the contribution of different sentiment features to model predictions.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews the latest research on sentiment analysis and stock market forecasting. Section 3 presents the proposed methodology, including data preprocessing, sentiment feature engineering, and reinforcement learning setup. Section 4 reports experimental results, while Section 5 discusses key findings and future research directions.

In [4], the authors present a hybrid financial advisory system that combines social media sentiment analysis, price forecasting, and risk assessment to enhance stock market predictions. The system employs a two-part architecture:
- LSTM predicts short-term stock prices with an accuracy of 76.2%.
- XGBoost, enhanced through PCA-based feature selection and parameter tuning, classifies future stock trends with 94.1% accuracy, achieving strong F1 scores across both rising and falling price categories.

A novel 4-level risk analysis framework was introduced based on the alignment or conflict between the LSTM and XGBoost predictions—offering investors a confidence measure with each forecast. This risk-aware output fills a major gap in existing financial models.
A 4-level risk scale was created based on agreement or disagreement between XGBoost and LSTM:

- Level 1 (low risk): Both models predict price rise

- Level 4 (high risk): Both models predict price drop

- Mixed signals fall into Level 2 or 3

This hybrid decision framework added a confidence indicator for each prediction. The study emphasizes that sentiment extracted from Reddit—despite potential noise—can reliably enhance market insight when combined with traditional metrics. It also highlights the model’s adaptability, suggesting potential applications in other financial domains like real estate. This work marks a significant advancement in NLP-based stock forecasting by merging predictive accuracy with practical risk evaluation—offering a more complete tool for modern investment decision-making.

In [7], the authors introduce Senti-DD, a novel sentiment lexicon construction framework tailored for the financial domain, addressing the limitation of traditional sentiment lexicons that ignore context and directionality. This method accounts for how sentiment shifts depending on whether financial indicators are increasing or decreasing (e.g., “profit” being positive in “profit increased” but negative in “profit decreased”).

🧠 Core Contributions
Senti-DD Lexicon: Automatically built by calculating Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) between terms and directional indicators (e.g., up, down).

Each entry in the lexicon is a pair: a directional word + direction-dependent word.

Integrated as a plug-in extension to the well-known Loughran-McDonald Word List.

📊 Experimental Results
Outperformed traditional lexicons like SentiWordNet, TextBlob, VADER, and LM across four financial datasets.

Achieved significantly higher F1-scores, especially in correctly classifying negative sentiment, where directionality plays a crucial role.

Corrected misclassifications of sentences that were previously labeled neutral due to the absence of traditional polarity words.

Qualitative evaluation confirmed the lexicon’s ability to extract meaningful financial sentiment words (e.g., revenue, investment, cost) and assign the correct polarity based on directional context.

🔬 Case Example
“Profit for the period was EUR 10.9 million, down from EUR 14.3 million.”

Loughran-McDonald alone misclassified this as neutral.

LM+Senti-DD correctly identified it as negative, thanks to the “profit + down” pairing.

🧩 Conclusion & Future Directions
The study demonstrates that context-aware lexicons, like Senti-DD, can dramatically improve financial sentiment classification. It provides an efficient and scalable alternative to manual annotation by leveraging patterns in domain-specific corpora.

The authors propose expanding this framework to other domains such as consumer reviews, where direction-sensitive sentiment (e.g., definition + high = positive, definition + low = negative) also applies. They also highlight the importance of larger labeled datasets and more analyst-style documents to further refine sentiment detection.
